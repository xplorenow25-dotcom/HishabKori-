How to Pay Tax in Bangladesh in 2026: A Complete Step-by-Step Guide

Paying income tax in Bangladesh can feel complicated if you are filing for the first time. The good news is that individual taxpayers can complete much of the process online through the National Board of Revenue (NBR) e-Return system. This guide explains how to check whether you need to file, estimate your tax, prepare documents, pay any outstanding amount, and submit your return for the 2026--2027 assessment year.

Important: This article focuses on personal income tax, not VAT, customs duty, or company tax. Tax rules can change, so confirm the latest instructions on the NBR website before filing.

1. Understand the tax year in Bangladesh

Bangladesh uses an income year and an assessment year. For the 2026--2027 assessment year, the income year for most individual taxpayers is generally 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026. Your return reports income earned during that income year, along with applicable investments, assets, liabilities, tax deducted at source, and tax payments.

2. Who needs to pay or file income tax?

Your filing obligation depends on your income, taxpayer status, and the requirements of the Income Tax Act, 2023, as amended. A return may be required when your income exceeds the tax-free threshold, when you were assessed in a recent year, or when you fall into a category legally required to file. Certain government employees, company directors, business owners, and other taxpayers may have separate filing obligations.

For 2026--2027, NBR says online filing is mandatory for individual taxpayers, with stated exceptions including taxpayers aged 65 or above, people who are physically unable or have special needs, Bangladeshis living abroad, and legal representatives filing for a deceased taxpayer. Check the latest NBR notice to confirm whether an exception applies to you.

Official resources: NBR Income Tax FAQ and NBR Income Tax Acts.

3. Know the 2026--2027 individual tax-free threshold and slabs

For the 2026--2027 assessment year, the published NBR budget schedule lists these thresholds for individuals:

General individual taxpayer: Tk 375,000

Women and taxpayers aged 65 or above: Tk 425,000

Persons with disabilities: Tk 500,000

Third-gender taxpayers: Tk 500,000

Gazetted war-wounded freedom fighters and gazetted July Warriors: Tk 525,000

Additional relief may apply to parents or legal guardians of a child with a disability, subject to the applicable rules.

For a general individual taxpayer, the published progressive rates for assessment year 2026--2027 are:

Taxable income band          Rate

First Tk 375,000               0% Next Tk 300,000               10% Next Tk 400,000               15% Next Tk 500,000               20% Next Tk 2,000,000             25% Remaining taxable income      30%

These are marginal rates: each rate applies only to the portion of income within that band, not to your entire income. Special rules, minimum tax, surcharges, rebates, exemptions, and other provisions may affect the final amount. Check the latest NBR budget document and current tax circular before relying on a calculation.

Simple example

Suppose a general taxpayer has Tk 800,000 of taxable income and no adjustment that changes the calculation. The first Tk 375,000 is taxed at 0%; the next Tk 300,000 at 10% equals Tk 30,000; and the remaining Tk 125,000 at 15% equals Tk 18,750. The illustrative tax is therefore Tk 48,750 before considering any applicable rebate, minimum tax, surcharge, or other adjustment.

This is an illustration, not a final assessment. Your taxable income is not always the same as your gross salary or total cash received.

4. Prepare the information and documents

Before opening the e-Return portal, gather the information relevant to your circumstances:

Your e-TIN and identification details.

Salary certificate, payslips, and employer-provided tax deduction statements.

Records of business, professional, rental, agricultural, investment, or other income, where applicable.

Bank interest certificates, dividend statements, and evidence of tax deducted at source.

Eligible investment or donation documents if you intend to claim a permitted tax rebate.

Information about assets, liabilities, expenses, and other disclosures requested in the return.

Receipts or challans for advance tax and tax already paid.

5. Register or sign in to the NBR e-Return system

Visit the official NBR e-Return portal. If you do not have an e-TIN, use the official e-TIN registration service linked from NBR. If you already have one, follow the portal's current registration or sign-in instructions. The system may require identity verification and a mobile number associated with your taxpayer record.

Once signed in, choose the 2026--2027 assessment year and check the income year shown on the form. Enter your taxpayer information carefully. Then complete the relevant income sections---for example, employment, rent, business, capital gains, or other sources---and fill in the tax, investment, assets, and liabilities sections that apply to you.

6. Calculate the tax and account for tax already paid

Review the calculation produced by the portal. Compare it with your own records and check that all income sources are included. Then account for any tax already deducted by an employer, bank, client, or other payer, as well as any eligible advance tax payments.

Your final amount due may be affected by tax rebates, minimum tax rules, surcharge, special income treatment, and other provisions. If your income comes from several sources, you own a business, have foreign income, or are uncertain about eligibility for a tax benefit, consider getting advice from a qualified tax professional.

7. How to pay income tax online

If the return shows additional tax payable, use the payment options currently available through the NBR system. NBR lists e-Payment and a-Challan among its official services. The government a-Challan portal is available at [achallan.gov.bd](http://achallan.gov.bd).

In general:

Confirm the tax amount and the correct tax head or payment category shown by the official system.

Choose an available payment method, such as the supported online payment or a-Challan option.

Enter the required taxpayer and assessment details carefully. Check the TIN, assessment year, amount, and payment purpose before submitting.

Complete the payment through the payment provider or official portal.

Save the receipt, transaction ID, or challan reference.

Return to the e-Return system and verify that the payment is reflected or record it as instructed.

8. Submit the return and save proof

After entering your income, tax credits, payments, and required disclosures, carefully review the summary. Correct any missing fields or mismatched amounts. Submit the return using the portal's final submission step and wait for confirmation.

Download or save the acknowledgement, return copy, tax computation, and any available proof-of-submission certificate. NBR's e-Return services include options for filing and return verification. Keep these records with your supporting documents.

Submitting a return and paying tax are related but different tasks. If tax is due, make sure payment is completed as required; if no additional tax is due, you may still need to submit the return and retain proof.

9. What is the deadline for filing in 2026?

NBR's general guidance identifies 30 November as the tax day for many individual taxpayers, but special cases and official extensions can change the applicable deadline. For the 2026--2027 assessment year, check the latest NBR notice before assuming a date. Do not rely on an old article or last year's deadline.

10. Common mistakes to avoid

Using the wrong assessment year: Check that the return matches the income year you are reporting.

Leaving out income: Include all relevant income sources, not only salary.

Entering tax deducted at source incorrectly: Match entries against certificates and records.

Assuming below-threshold income means no return is needed: Verify your filing obligation separately.

Choosing the wrong payment category: Check the official tax head, TIN, and assessment year before paying.

Failing to save proof: Keep your submitted return, acknowledgement, and payment receipts.

Relying on unofficial advice: Use current NBR notices and the applicable law, especially when rules change.

Final checklist

Before you finish, make sure you have selected the correct assessment year, reported all applicable income, checked eligible credits and rebates, paid any amount due, submitted the return, and saved the confirmation. Use the official NBR website, e-Return portal, and a-Challan service for the latest steps.
